# Recognize it
Signs of a heart attack: a tight pain in the chest, which can spread to the arm, shoulder, neck, jaw or stomach. Sometimes also difficulty breathing, sweating, nausea, stomach pain.

Feeling unwell can also come from heat or lack of water: see the "Heat" sheet.

# The person responds
Don't play it down, even if they say it's nothing serious: they need help straight away.

Ask them these questions: how long has this been going on, is it the first time, have they already been hospitalized or do they take medication for it?

Call 15 (SAMU, emergency medical service), or ask someone to do it. If you are alone, do it yourself.

Help them rest in a comfortable position: lying down, half-sitting, or sitting if they prefer. Check regularly that they are conscious and breathing normally.

# The person doesn't respond
- First, loosen their collar, tie or belt. Put one hand on their forehead and gently tilt their head back. With two fingers of your other hand, lift their chin.
- Next, check their breathing for 10 seconds: look whether the chest rises, listen near their mouth, feel for breath on your cheek.
- Then have someone alert the emergency services: 15 or 18. If you are alone, go for help or call yourself.

If they are breathing normally: put them in the recovery position (position latérale de sécurité, PLS), unless you suspect a trauma injury (in that case call the emergency services and follow their advice):
- Remove their glasses and straighten their legs in line with the body.
- Place the arm nearest to you at a right angle, elbow bent, palm up.
- Bring their other arm against their ear, on your side, and keep the palms against each other.
- Grab their far leg just behind the knee and raise it, keeping the foot on the ground.
- Roll them towards you by pulling on the leg, until their knee touches the ground.
- Gently slide your hand out from under their head while holding the elbow, without moving the head.
- Adjust the top leg so that the hip and knee form a right angle.
- Open their mouth with your thumb and index finger, without moving the head, so fluids can drain.
- Check regularly that their breathing stays normal.

If they are not breathing, or not normally, it is a cardiac arrest:
- Have someone call 15 or 18 and bring a defibrillator if there is one. If you are alone, do it yourself.
- Lay them on their back, on a hard surface. Kneel beside them.
- Place the heel of one hand in the centre of their bare chest, the other hand on top. Don't press on the ribs or the bottom of the breastbone.
- Arms straight, shoulders directly above their chest, push the breastbone down 5 to 6 cm. Let the chest come all the way back up between each push. Do 30 compressions, at about 2 per second.
- If you know how, then give 2 mouth-to-mouth rescue breaths, then start 30 compressions again. If you can't give breaths, carry on with compressions only.
- As soon as the defibrillator arrives, switch it on and follow its voice instructions.
- Keep going until help arrives or until they breathe normally.

# If the person is choking
They are conscious but can't speak, breathe or cough. They may wheeze or try to cough silently.

- Stand to the side and slightly behind them. Lean them forward, supporting their chest with one hand.
- Give up to 5 slaps between the shoulder blades. After each slap, check whether the object has come out.
- If not, stand behind them, put your arms around the top of their stomach and lean them forward. Place a closed fist between the navel and the bottom of the breastbone, held by your other hand. Pull hard towards you and upwards, up to 5 times.
- If the object doesn't come out, alternate 5 back slaps and 5 abdominal thrusts.
- If they lose consciousness, lay them down gently, alert the emergency services straight away and start chest compressions as for a cardiac arrest.

# What not to do
- Don't play down someone feeling unwell because they say they're fine.
- Don't press on the soft part under the chin of an unconscious person.
- Don't put someone you suspect has a trauma injury in the recovery position: call the emergency services and follow their advice.

# When to call for help
Call 15, 18 or 112 in all these cases:
- a tight pain in the chest,
- a person who doesn't respond,
- a person who isn't breathing, or not normally,
- a person who is choking and isn't helped by the slaps and thrusts.
