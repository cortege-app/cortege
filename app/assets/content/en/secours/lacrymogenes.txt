# Recognize it
Tear gases are irritant agents. The most common one, CS, irritates the eyes, the airways and the skin. The signs:

- eyes: watering, redness, sometimes sensitivity to light,
- skin: burning, stinging, sometimes blisters if the product stays on the skin,
- nose and throat: runny nose, coughing, sneezing, wheezing, shortness of breath, chest pain,
- sometimes: nausea, vomiting, headaches.

These effects wear off within a few hours. Most people recover within half an hour.

# First steps
- Leave the area, towards fresh air. Leaving the area is usually enough to get your breathing back to normal quickly.
- Eyes: rinse thoroughly, with clean water or saline solution, eyelids held open, for at least 10 minutes. For a chemical in the eye, the INRS recommends 15 minutes.
- Skin: remove the product by brushing, washing or rinsing with plenty of water. Water can briefly make the pain worse, but it still works.

# What not to do
- Don't rub your eyes with your fingers: it can make the irritation worse (advice from an ophthalmologist at Cochin hospital reported by the press).
- Milk or Coca-Cola are no more effective than water. Advice to use Maalox, vinegar or lemon is not backed by any medical source consulted.

! To be checked by first aiders: the two points above come from the press, not from an official source.

# Special cases
Contact lenses: if you wear them, take them out if you can, with clean hands, before rinsing. The INRS advises soft lens wearers to wear glasses when they may be exposed to aerosols.

Asthma and lungs: some people get irritated airways, with bouts of breathlessness similar to asthma. People with a chronic eye, skin or lung condition should avoid exposure.

If the symptoms last or other signs appear, see a doctor.
